# 01 · The challenge

**Organisers:** Hack-Nation with the World Bank Group (Digital & AI Vice Presidency), MIT Club of Northern California, MIT Club of Germany.
**Guiding question:** What does localizing AI development mean for you?

## Persona: Noor (fictional)
- 38, farms 2 ha: coffee on the upper slope, maize and beans below; cooperative member for 11 years.
- Basic phone for calls, SMS, mobile money; daughter's smartphone only at weekends.
- No Wi-Fi; buys 3G bundles when needed. Out on the field all day, phone stays at home.
- Speaks her local language; the national language only when needed.
- Agriculture problem: yields falling for unknown reasons; extension officer visits about twice a year; no independent price reference at harvest.

## Hard rules
1. Runs on a device the user already has.
2. Core feature works offline.
3. Model files small enough to side-load or send over a weak connection.
4. At least one interaction in a named local language; expect questions about a less-supported language.
5. Human in the loop; the tool flags uncertainty and never acts for the user.
6. Avoid hallucinations.

## Deliverables (end of weekend)
1. Prototype (code or link).
2. Video, 2–5 min (no video = no shortlist): one-sentence problem statement, AI capabilities and guardrails, demo, where the tool sits in the user's day (+ tech stack), "our take" on localizing AI.

## Judging
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Built solution (Small AI fidelity) | 25% |
| Development relevance and impact | 20% |
| Data grounding | 15% |
| Evidence it works | 15% |
| Clarity, design, value of AI | 15% |
| Scalability, replicability, what happens next | 10% |
| Responsible AI, data and safety | Pass/fail |

Finance and business are **not** required deliverables; they support "what happens next" and relevance.
